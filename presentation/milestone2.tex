\begin{frame}{From the previous presentation}
  \footnotesize
  \hd{Original framing.} \emph{How does the topology of the global maritime oil trade network shape the dynamics of oil prices?}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
    \item \hd{Q1.} Can network topology improve oil-price forecasts beyond classical time-series baselines?
    \item \hd{Q2.} How does a maritime disruption propagate into oil prices across horizons, and how long does its effect linger?
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \hd{Why the pivot to causal SCM.}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
    \item We agreed to focus on \hd{causal} estimation rather than forecasting --- forecasting is hard and crowded; a credible counterfactual on a specific disruption is more tractable and policy-relevant.
    \item We reviewed candidate causal methods: event studies, DiD, network-shock propagation, synthetic control.
    \item \hd{SCM} selected: cleanest combination of \hd{inferential machinery} (in-space placebo) and \hd{interpretable result} (donor-weighted counterfactual on the observed price), and it directly produces an ATT estimate rather than a shock decomposition.
  \end{itemize}
  \normalsize
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[shrink=10]{Validation results --- in-space placebo}
  \footnotesize
  \hd{Procedure} \src{Abadie 2010}: refit the SCM treating each donor as treated. \begin{frame}{Headline result --- ATT estimate}
  \footnotesize
  \hd{Why ATT?} One treated unit (Brent) $\rightarrow$ effect \emph{for that unit}, not a population ATE \src{Abadie 2010, 2021}. Mean gap over the post-event window --- integrates path-reversion, robust to single-day liquidity jumps.

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}l c c@{}}
    \toprule
    \hd{Model}     & \hd{Hormuz 2026} & \hd{Russia 2022} \\
    \midrule
    Convex SCM         & $+38.6\%$ & $+29.6\%$ \\
    ASCM               & $+43.7\%$ & $+13.3\%$ \\
    Elastic-net        & $+49.8\%$ & $+21.7\%$ \\
    XGBoost            & $+36.8\%$ & $+36.8\%$ \\
    Bayesian Ridge     & $+43.6\%$ & $+0.5\%$ \smalltext{(deg.)} \\
    \midrule
    \hd{Ensemble median} & \textcolor{accent}{\hd{$+43.6\%$}} & \textcolor{accent}{\hd{$+21.7\%$}} \\
    IQR                  & $[+38.6,\ +43.7]$ & $[+13.3,\ +29.6]$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \hd{Reading.} Hormuz $+43.6\%$ with tight IQR --- place against Dallas Fed / OIES / IEA scenarios. Russia $+21.7\%$ within $\sim$5.7 pp of STEO Feb-22 ATT (synth \$88.92 vs STEO \$84.94, $\Delta$ \$3.98/bbl); Bayesian Ridge degenerate, IQR excludes it.

\begin{frame}[shrink=5]{Part 2 --- future directions}
  \scriptsize
  \vspace{0.15em}

  Three directions build directly on the Brent SCM result established in Part~1.


  \vspace{0.5em}
  \hd{1 \ \ LNG market causal estimation (JKM, TTF)}

  \textcolor{muted} {Instead of building the whole story around Brent, change the focus on commodity prices in general which were affected by the disruption. Apply the same SCM design to JKM Asian LNG spot price and/or TTF European LNG spot price.
  Identify the Hormuz LNG premium paid by Asian buyers vs a cross-asset counterfactual.}
  First causal estimate of the Hormuz effect on Asian LNG pricing.
  Same methodology, different treated asset.



  \vspace{0.4em}
  \hd{2 \ \ Local projections (LP) across horizons}
  
  \textcolor{muted}{Estimate the Brent treatment effect at
  $h = 1, 5, 14, 30$ days.}
  Produces a horizon-by-horizon impulse response: how fast the effect builds
  and how long it persists.
  SCM gives the average ATT over a window; LP gives the \emph{shape} of the
  path --- different and complementary information.

  
  \vspace{0.5em}
  \hd{3 \ \ Retail fuel and CPI pass-through}
  
  \textcolor{muted}{Estimate what share of the $\sim$44\% Brent premium
  passed through to retail gasoline and headline CPI in oil-importing economies.}
  Converts the upstream ATT into a consumer-price impact estimate.
  The Brent ATT is the upstream input; pass-through delivers the
  policy-relevant inflation number.
