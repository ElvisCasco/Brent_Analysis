\begin{abstract}
\noindent Roughly a fifth of the world's petroleum-liquids consumption transits the
Strait of Hormuz, so its disruption on 28 February 2026 was widely expected to move
global oil prices. Measuring that effect is not straightforward. Because Brent crude is a
unique global benchmark, no untreated control unit exists from which a counterfactual
price can be inferred directly. We construct that missing counterfactual with the
\emph{synthetic control method} (SCM), replacing the geographic donors of conventional
applications with a pool of 19 macro-financial series chosen to share Brent's exposure to
common global factors while staying off the oil-supply causal path. To keep the result
from hinging on any single modelling choice, the synthetic is the median of an ensemble
of five estimators with different inductive biases. We validate the design on the
Russian invasion of Ukraine of 24 February 2022, the closest comparable historical
oil-supply shock, where the same machinery recovers the documented price move, and estimate
the Hormuz effect on its own pre-window. Over the window to late June 2026 it yields an
average Brent premium of
roughly \SI{53}{\percent} (ensemble median, interquartile range \SIrange{48}{53}{\percent})
that is robust to placebo-based inference and a battery of robustness and contamination
checks, the latter indicating that any residual bias is conservative. The result is a
reduced-form, data-driven complement to the structural scenario estimates of energy
institutions, and the paper doubles as a transparent template for validating cross-asset
synthetic control under a single-unit geopolitical treatment.
\end{abstract}
