\section{Donor Selection}\label{sec:donoraudit}
% ===================================================================
This section applies the selection criteria of Section~\ref{sec:data} donor by donor.
It reports which candidates are retained and which are dropped under each event's
cleanliness audit, states the shared pool that results, gives the positive case for the
families excluded by construction, and closes with the empirical donor-level tests that
confirm the a-priori audit. The full per-event catalog of all 32 candidates, with each
one's factor loading and Clean/Mild/Heavy status for both events, is
Table~\ref{tab:donoraudit} in Appendix~\ref{app:donoraudit}; the discussion here is
organised by audit tier and by the reason for exclusion.

\subsection{The per-event cleanliness audit}
Each candidate is scored against the focal event on a three-level scale: \textbf{Clean}
(no direct supply or demand exposure to the event), \textbf{Mild} (a secondary or
terms-of-trade channel, bias possible but small), and \textbf{Heavy} (direct exposure, a
SUTVA violation). The two events stress the pool very differently. For \textbf{Russia
2022}, 13 of the 32 candidates carry an identifiable channel to the war: 6 are Heavy and
7 are Mild. The Heavy set is Wheat and Corn (Russia and Ukraine together supply roughly a
third of world wheat exports and an eighth of corn), Palladium (Russia is around
40\,\% of supply), the euro and the dollar basket (the European energy-crisis
pass-through dominates both), and Bitcoin (the sanctions-evasion narrative of March
2022). The Mild set is Copper, Iron Ore, Soybeans, and EM equities (terms-of-trade and
EM-growth exposure), sterling (UK gas exposure), the US 10-year yield (the
European-inflation channel), and Cotton (the indirect oil$\to$polyester substitution
channel). For \textbf{Hormuz 2026} the audit is far cleaner: no candidate is physically
routed through the Strait, and only Cotton is flagged Mild, again on the oil$\to$polyester
channel rather than any Gulf-trade exposure.

\subsection{The retained shared pool}
The shared pool is the Russia strict-clean set, the 19 candidates rated Clean for the
more demanding event (Table~\ref{tab:donors}). Retaining the Russia-clean set drops the
13 Russia-flagged candidates (the 6 Heavy and 7 Mild above); because that set is a subset
of the Hormuz-clean set, all 19 stay clean for both events. Cotton illustrates the logic:
it is Mild for both events, so even though Hormuz alone would tolerate it under a
permissive variant, it is excluded on the strength of the Russia audit and its indirect
channel never enters the shared pool. One retained donor, VIX, is borderline. A
full-history Bai--Perron test \citep{baiperron1998} dates a single break in its
relationship with Brent on 2022-01-07, about seven weeks \emph{before} the invasion. We
read that as a loading-stability signal rather than a SUTVA violation, because it does not
recur when the test is run on the analysis window alone, and a sensitivity check shows
that keeping or dropping VIX moves the ensemble premium by less than one percentage point.
We therefore retain VIX, which keeps the volatility factor represented.

\subsection{Candidates excluded by construction}
Several families never reach the per-event audit because criterion (ii) rules them out a
priori. The \emph{energy complex} (WTI, Henry Hub natural gas, refined products, EIA
ex-SPR inventories) has the highest pre-period correlation with Brent and would give the
lowest pre-period RMSPE, yet every member is driven by the same oil-specific shock $o_t$
we are trying to measure, so each would mechanically shrink the estimated premium toward
zero. \emph{Petrocurrencies} (CAD, NOK, BRL, COP, MYR, and the suspended RUB) are
excluded for the same reason in FX form: their value is partly set by oil through the
exporter's terms of trade, which places them on the oil causal path. \emph{Unstable or
idiosyncratic FX} (TRY, ARS, EGP) are dropped because their volatility is dominated by
domestic crisis dynamics that add noise rather than shared-factor signal.
\emph{Energy-heavy equity indices} (DAX, FTSE~100, TSX~60) carry direct energy-sector or
European-energy-crisis exposure, and KOSPI and Hang Seng are redundant with the KRW and
CNY factors. \emph{Thin or discontinuous commodities} (cocoa, orange juice, lean hogs,
lumber) are too idiosyncratic or break contract continuity in the pre-period. Finally,
MSCI World is dropped as redundant with the S\&P 500, with which it correlates above 0.95.
Appendix~\ref{app:donoraudit} records each rejected family with its specific reason.

\subsection{Empirical confirmation of the audit}
The a-priori audit is the primary identification defence; the donor-level battery of
Section~\ref{sec:methods} is its empirical check, and the two agree closely. For
\textbf{Hormuz 2026}, 31 of the 32 candidates agree between the qualitative audit and the
model-agnostic tests at $T_0$; only Cotton differs, and its Mild rating is an economic
causal-channel call that the contemporaneous tests are not expected to reproduce for an
indirect, lagged channel. For \textbf{Russia 2022} the tests confirm the broad picture
and add a single flag on CNY, which is attributable to the concurrent China zero-COVID
dynamics rather than to the war. The regime-stability tests behave consistently: the only
within-pre-window relationship break the Bai--Perron procedure dates among retained
donors is the VIX break of 2022-01-07 discussed above, which falls before $T_0$ and is
treated as informative rather than exclusionary. The empirical battery therefore
corroborates the cleanliness of the retained pool without overturning any audit call.
